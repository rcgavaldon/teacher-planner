Common mistake
'Negative feedback' means the response goes in the OPPOSITE direction of the change — it REVERSES it. Sweating when hot is negative feedback, and it's good for you.

Concept 1 — the six levels of organization

How does the body build from atoms up to a whole person?

  1. Atoms join into molecules — the chemical level.
  2. Molecules build a cell, the smallest unit of life.
  3. Many similar cells form a tissue; several tissues form an organ.
  4. Organs that share a job form an organ system; all systems together make the organism.

chemical → cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism

Concept 2 — homeostasis by negative feedback

You get too hot. How does your body pull you back to normal?

  1. A change pushes a variable off its set point — the stimulus.
  2. A receptor detects the change and sends the signal in.
  3. The control center (often the brain) compares it to the set point.
  4. An effector makes a response that reverses the change — bringing you back to normal.

stimulus → receptor → control center → effector → response (reverses the change)

Check your understanding
1.

Put in order, smallest to largest: organ, cell, organism, tissue, organ system, chemical.

chemical, cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism

2.

Shivering when you get cold — negative or positive feedback? Explain in one sentence.

Negative feedback — shivering makes heat to REVERSE the drop in temperature, back toward the set point.

3.

Why is the heart an organ and not a tissue?

It is made of two or more different tissues working together, which defines an organ.

Summary
Anatomy is structure, physiology is function. The body builds chemical to organism, and negative feedback keeps it stable by reversing changes.

Explain & apply

  2. Stimulus = body temp rises; receptor = skin/hypothalamus senses it; control center = hypothalamus (brain) compares to the set point; effector = sweat glands produce sweat. It is NEGATIVE feedback because the response (cooling) reverses the original change (heating).

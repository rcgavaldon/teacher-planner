{\small Pre-Calculus \textbar\ Guided notes --- we fill these in together}\vspace{2pt}\hrule\vspace{7pt}
\textbf{Vocabulary}\\[5pt]
\textbf{Increasing interval:} the set of \bl[1cm]{$x$}-values where the graph goes \bl[1.4cm]{up} as you move left to right.\\[10pt]
\textbf{Decreasing interval:} the set of $x$-values where the graph goes \bl[1.6cm]{down} as you move left to right.\\[10pt]
\textbf{Relative max/min:} a \bl[2.6cm]{turning point} of the graph; an upward-opening parabola has a \bl[2cm]{minimum}, a downward-opening parabola has a \bl[2.2cm]{maximum}.\\[12pt]
\textbf{We do together.}\quad For $y=(x-2)^{2}-3$, find the vertex, state max or min, and give the increasing/decreasing intervals.\\[6pt]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.},leftmargin=3.1em,itemsep=9pt]
  \item Vertex form $a(x-h)^2+k$ gives the vertex directly: \bl[2cm]{$(2,-3)$}.
  \item The leading coefficient is $+1$, which is positive, so the parabola opens \bl[1.2cm]{up}, making the vertex a \bl[2cm]{minimum}.
  \item The graph falls before the vertex and rises after: decreasing on \bl[2cm]{$x<2$}, increasing on \bl[2cm]{$x>2$}.
\end{enumerate}\vspace{8pt}

\textbf{You Try.}\ Solve each. Show every step, then name the answer.\\[6pt]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*)},leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=1.4cm]
  \item For $y=-x^{2}+4$, state whether the vertex is a max or min, and give the intervals. \hfill \ifkey\textcolor{blue!65!black}{vertex $(0,4)$ is a maximum; increasing on $x<0$, decreasing on $x>0$}\fi
  \item For $y=(x+1)^{2}$, give the vertex and the increasing interval. \hfill \ifkey\textcolor{blue!65!black}{vertex $(-1,0)$; increasing on $x>-1$}\fi
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{4pt}\hrule\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Summary (fill in):} Opening \bl[1cm]{up} gives a minimum; opening \bl[1.4cm]{down} gives a maximum. The graph \bl[2.4cm]{decreases} before the turning point and \bl[2.2cm]{increases} after it (for an upward parabola).

\textbf{Vocabulary}\\[5pt]
\textbf{Axis of Symmetry:} The vertical line \bl[2cm]{$x=h$} passing through the vertex of a parabola.\\[10pt]
\textbf{y-intercept:} The point where a graph crosses the y-axis, found by substituting \bl[2cm]{$x=0$} into the equation.\\[12pt]
\textbf{We do together.}\quad Graph $y=(x-3)^2-2$ using vertex, axis of symmetry, and y-intercept.\\[6pt]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.},leftmargin=3.1em,itemsep=9pt]
  \item Vertex: $(h,k)=$ \bl[2.5cm]{$(3,-2)$}.
  \item Axis of symmetry: $x=$ \bl[1cm]{$3$}.
  \item y-intercept: substitute $x=0$: $y=(0-3)^2-2=9-2=$ \bl[1.5cm]{$7$}, giving point $(0,7)$.
  \item Mirror point across $x=3$: since $(0,7)$ is 3 units left of the axis, the mirror point is 3 units right: \bl[2.5cm]{$(6,7)$}.
  \item Plot $(3,-2)$, $(0,7)$, and $(6,7)$, then sketch the \bl[2.5cm]{U-shaped} curve.
\end{enumerate}\vspace{8pt}

\textbf{Summary (fill in):} To graph a quadratic in vertex form, first plot the vertex $(h,k)$, then draw the axis of symmetry \bl[2cm]{$x=h$}. Find the y-intercept by substituting \bl[2cm]{$x=0$}, then reflect that point across the axis of symmetry to get a matching point, and connect all the points with a smooth curve.

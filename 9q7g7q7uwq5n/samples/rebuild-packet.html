Multiplying and Dividing Rational Expressions

Multiply and divide rational expressions, simplify, and state restrictions.

01 · Project

Today's addition — Multiply & Divide Rational Expressions

New concept1 / 4

Same four moves, every problem

  • Factor every top and bottom completely (GCF, difference of squares, trinomials).
  • Dividing? Flip the second fraction FIRST — then it's a multiply problem.
  • Cancel matching factors, then simplify.
  • Restrictions come from the ORIGINAL denominators — before anything cancels.
Worked example2 / 4

We do: divide with a trinomial

Worked example
$\dfrac{2x^2+9x+4}{3x}\div\dfrac{2x+1}{x}$
1. Flip: $\cdot\,\dfrac{x}{2x+1}$.
2. Factor: $2x^2+9x+4=(2x+1)(x+4)$.
3. Cancel $(2x+1)$ and $x$: $\dfrac{x+4}{3}$.
4. Restrictions: $x\neq 0,\,-\tfrac12$.
Watch out3 / 4

The restriction trap

Common mistake
Restrictions come from the ORIGINAL denominators — including factors that later cancel, and the divisor's TOP when you divide.
If you read them off the simplified answer, you miss values like $x=-2$ in Example 1.
Keep in mind
Write the restrictions down at the FACTOR step, before you cancel anything.

02 · Model on the board

Worked procedure — “I do”

1

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2+5x+6}{x}\cdot\dfrac{x}{x+2}$ and state restrictions.

  1. Factor: $x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)$.

  2. Write as one fraction: $\dfrac{(x+2)(x+3)\cdot x}{x\cdot(x+2)}$.

  3. Cancel $(x+2)$ and $x$.

  4. State restrictions from the original denominators: $x\neq 0,-2$.

Answer

$x+3$, $x\neq 0,-2$

2

Simplify $\dfrac{2x^2+9x+4}{3x}\div\dfrac{2x+1}{x}$ and state restrictions.

  1. Multiply by the reciprocal: $\dfrac{2x^2+9x+4}{3x}\cdot\dfrac{x}{2x+1}$.

  2. Factor: $2x^2+9x+4=(2x+1)(x+4)$.

  3. Cancel $(2x+1)$ and $x$: $\dfrac{x+4}{3}$.

  4. Restrictions: $3x\neq 0$, divisor top $2x+1\neq 0$, divisor bottom $x\neq 0$ → $x\neq 0,-\tfrac12$.

Answer

$\dfrac{x+4}{3}$, $x\neq 0,-\tfrac12$

Vocabulary
Rational expression — a fraction whose top and bottom are polynomials
Restriction — an $x$-value that makes an ORIGINAL denominator equal zero
Reciprocal — the flipped fraction — dividing means multiplying by the reciprocal
Example 1 · Type 1 — multiply

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2+5x+6}{x}\cdot\dfrac{x}{x+2}$ and state restrictions.

  1. Factor every top and bottom: $x^2+5x+6=$ $(x+2)(x+3)$
  2. Cancel common factors: the $(x+2)$ pair and the $x$ pair cancel
  3. Simplified result: $x+3$
  4. Restrictions from ORIGINAL denominators: $x\neq$ $0,\,-2$

$x+3$, $x\neq 0,-2$

Example 2 · Type 2 — divide (flip first)

Simplify $\dfrac{2x^2+9x+4}{3x}\div\dfrac{2x+1}{x}$ and state restrictions.

  1. Flip the divisor and multiply: $\div\dfrac{2x+1}{x}$ becomes $\cdot$ $\dfrac{x}{2x+1}$
  2. Factor: $2x^2+9x+4=$ $(2x+1)(x+4)$
  3. Cancel $(2x+1)$ and $x$: result $\dfrac{x+4}{3}$
  4. Restrictions (original denominators AND the divisor's top): $x\neq$ $0,\,-\tfrac12$

$\dfrac{x+4}{3}$, $x\neq 0,-\tfrac12$

Answer key

Multiply, simplify, and state restrictions

  1. $\dfrac{4x}{3(x+4)}$; restrictions $x\neq 4,\,-4$

  2. $\dfrac{2m}{m+5}$; restrictions $m\neq 0,\,-5$ — ⚠ the sheet's key shows $\tfrac{2}{m+5}$, which drops an $m$: $\tfrac{8m}{m(m+5)}\cdot\tfrac{m}{4}=\tfrac{8m^2}{4m(m+5)}=\tfrac{2m}{m+5}$

  3. $3(x+2)$; restrictions $x\neq 0,\,-5$

  4. $x+3$; restrictions $x\neq 0,\,-\tfrac12$

  5. $2y^{2}$; restriction $y\neq 0$

  6. $\dfrac{x}{x+1}$; restrictions $x\neq 0,\,-1$

Divide, simplify, and state restrictions

  1. $\dfrac{x+5}{5}$; restrictions $x\neq 0,\,5$

  2. $2(x+4)$; restrictions $x\neq 0,\,-2$

  3. $x+3$; restrictions $x\neq 0,\,-\tfrac23$

  4. $\dfrac{a}{a+1}$; restrictions $a\neq 0,\,-1$ — ⚠ the sheet's key shows $\tfrac{1}{a+1}$, which drops an $a$: $\tfrac{3a}{a(a+1)}\cdot\tfrac{a}{3}=\tfrac{3a\cdot a}{3a(a+1)}=\tfrac{a}{a+1}$

  5. $\dfrac{8y}{(y-2)(y+2)^{2}}$; restrictions $y\neq 2,\,-2$ — ⚠ the sheet's key shows $\tfrac{2}{y-2}$; flipping gives $\tfrac{2y}{(y-2)(y+2)}\cdot\tfrac{4}{y+2}=\tfrac{8y}{(y-2)(y+2)^2}$ and nothing else cancels

  6. $2$; restriction $p\neq 0$
